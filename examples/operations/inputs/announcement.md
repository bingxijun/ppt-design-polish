# Dispatch pilot | operations review

This is a fictional operational review for an open-source presentation example. All counts, rates, dates and observations are invented for demonstration. It is not evidence of a real business outcome.

A four-week dispatch pilot handled 2,400 orders at one warehouse. The baseline was a separate 2,000-order period. The periods had different order mixes, so the comparison does not establish a causal effect. The decision is whether to extend observation for two more weeks before any wider rollout.

On-time dispatch was 88% in the baseline and 95% in week 4. Across the four pilot weeks, on-time dispatch was 88%, 91%, 94% and 95%. On-time means handed to the carrier before the agreed cutoff, with cancelled orders excluded. These percentages are illustrative and rounded; per-week denominators are not provided.

The comparison table uses baseline and week 4 only. On-time dispatch: 88% and 95%, defined as carrier handover before cutoff. Median pick time: 14 min and 11 min, measured from pick start to pick complete. Exception rate: 6.0% and 4.5%, meaning orders needing manual intervention. Counts of exceptions and the distribution of pick times are not provided.

Three unresolved issues affect rollout readiness. Carrier handover timestamps were missing for 40 pilot orders; the rate calculation excluded them and the missingness needs investigation. Weekend orders represented only 8% of the pilot volume, limiting generalisation to a full week. The pilot covered only one warehouse; no cross-site replication has been completed.

Next steps: extend observation for two more weeks; investigate the 40 missing timestamps with the operations team; include a fuller weekend sample. Wider rollout remains undecided. No owner names or completion dates have been supplied.
